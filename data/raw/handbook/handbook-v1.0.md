# Hollow Creek Poultry Co. Employee Handbook

> Hollow Creek Poultry Co. is a fictional company. This handbook is synthetic sample content for a portfolio project and is not legal advice.

## 1. About This Handbook

**Version 1.0 — effective November 1, 2026.** This handbook explains the policies, pay practices, leave and benefits that apply to all Hollow Creek Poultry Co. employees.

### 1.1 Purpose

This handbook is a guide to how we work together. It does not cover every situation. When a question is not answered here, contact your HR team (Section 2.3).

### 1.2 Not a contract

This handbook is not an employment contract. Employment with Hollow Creek Poultry Co. is at will: either the employee or the company may end it at any time, with or without cause or notice, as permitted by law.

### 1.3 Changes to this handbook

HR may update this handbook at any time. Each version shows its version number and effective date. The most recent version replaces all earlier versions.

### 1.4 Language

The English version of this handbook is the official, binding version. Spanish and Vietnamese versions are courtesy translations. If a translation differs from the English version, the English version applies.

### 1.5 State and federal law

Where a state or federal law gives employees more rights than this handbook, the law applies. State-specific rules are in Section 19.

## 3. Employment Classifications and Payroll Classes

Every employee belongs to one of five payroll classes, which set how often you are paid and whether you earn overtime.

### 3.1 Payroll classes

| Class | Who belongs | Pay type | Overtime |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Executive | Vice Presidents, Directors and the CEO | Salaried | Exempt |
| Biweekly | Non-executive employees with an annual salary above $75,000 | Salaried | Exempt |
| Weekly IT Help Desk | IT support employees not in the classes above | Hourly | Eligible |
| Weekly Production | Plant floor and sanitation employees | Hourly | Eligible |
| Drivers | Company drivers, each assigned to a plant | Hourly | Eligible |

### 3.2 Where each class works

All Executive and Biweekly employees work at the corporate office. Weekly Production and Drivers work at a plant. Weekly IT Help Desk employees work at a plant or at the corporate office.

### 3.3 Exempt and non-exempt

Exempt employees are paid a salary and do not earn overtime. Non-exempt employees are paid for every hour worked and earn overtime as described in Section 4.4.

### 3.4 Introductory period

The first 90 calendar days of employment are an introductory period. Some benefits, such as using vacation and sick time, begin after it ends. The introductory period does not change at-will employment (Section 1.2).

### 3.5 Changing classes

If your job or salary changes, HR will tell you if your payroll class changes. A new class takes effect on the first day of the next pay period.

## 4. Work Schedules, Shifts and Overtime

Office-based employees work 8:00 a.m. to 5:00 p.m., Monday through Friday; Weekly Production and Drivers work shifts or routes.

### 4.1 Standard schedule

Executive, Biweekly and Weekly IT Help Desk employees work 8:00 a.m. to 5:00 p.m., Monday through Friday, with a one-hour unpaid lunch. That is 8 paid hours a day.

### 4.2 Production shifts

Weekly Production employees work one fixed shift, Monday through Friday. Saturday work may be scheduled when production needs it. Each shift includes a 30-minute unpaid meal break and is 8 paid hours.

| Shift | Hours |
| --- | --- |
| 1st shift | 6:00 a.m. – 2:30 p.m. |
| 2nd shift | 3:00 p.m. – 11:30 p.m. |
| Sanitation | 11:30 p.m. – 6:00 a.m. |

### 4.3 Driver routes

Drivers work scheduled routes, usually 4 days a week at 10 hours a day. Your plant's transportation supervisor sets route schedules.

### 4.4 Overtime

All weekly payroll classes (Weekly IT Help Desk, Weekly Production and Drivers) earn overtime at 1.5 times their regular hourly rate for hours worked over 40 in a workweek. The workweek runs Sunday 12:00 a.m. to Saturday 11:59 p.m. Overtime must be approved in advance by your supervisor.

### 4.5 Shift swaps and schedule changes

Shift swaps and schedule changes are handled by your supervisor or manager. Talk to them directly; HR and the Roost HR Assistant cannot arrange swaps.

### 4.6 Timekeeping

Hourly employees must clock in and out at the time clock for every shift and meal break. Working off the clock is not allowed.

## 5. Pay Periods and Paydays

Weekly classes are paid every Friday; Executive and Biweekly employees are paid every other Friday, 26 times a year.

### 5.1 Pay schedule

| Payroll class | Pay period | Payday | Paychecks per year |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Weekly IT Help Desk, Weekly Production, Drivers | Sunday – Saturday (one week) | Friday after the week ends | 52 |
| Executive, Biweekly | Two weeks, Sunday – Saturday | Every other Friday | 26 |

### 5.2 When a payday falls on a holiday

If a payday falls on a company holiday (Section 12), you are paid on the business day before it.

### 5.3 Your first paycheck

Weekly employees receive their first paycheck on the second Friday after their start date. Executive and Biweekly employees receive theirs on the first biweekly payday after their first full pay period.

### 5.4 Questions about your pay

For questions about pay amounts, deductions, taxes, direct deposit, bank details or a possible pay error, contact your HR team (Section 2.3). The Roost HR Assistant can tell you when you will be paid, but it cannot show or discuss pay amounts or any financial information.

### 5.5 Reporting a pay error

If you believe your pay is wrong, report it to your HR team as soon as possible. Errors are corrected no later than the next regular payday. You will never be retaliated against for reporting a pay concern.

## 6. Vacation (PTO)

Every employee earns 10 vacation days a year, usable after 90 days and requested at least 14 days in advance.

### 6.1 How much you earn

All payroll classes earn 10 vacation days per calendar year. Vacation is tracked in hours: 10 times your scheduled shift length.

| Payroll class | Hours per year | Earned per paycheck |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Executive, Biweekly | 80 hours | 3.08 hours (26 paychecks) |
| Weekly IT Help Desk, Weekly Production | 80 hours | 1.54 hours (52 paychecks) |
| Drivers | 100 hours | 1.92 hours (52 paychecks) |

### 6.2 When you can use it

You start earning vacation on your first day. You can use it after you complete the 90-day introductory period (Section 3.4).

### 6.3 Smallest amount you can take

Vacation can be taken in half-day or full-day amounts. A half day is half of your scheduled shift.

### 6.4 Notice required

Submit vacation requests at least 14 calendar days before the first day off. Requests with less notice cannot be submitted through the Roost HR Assistant; talk to your supervisor directly, who may approve exceptions with HR.

### 6.5 Holiday production blackout

Weekly Production employees cannot schedule vacation from November 15 through December 31, our peak holiday production season. Exceptions require approval from the plant manager and plant HR.

### 6.6 Department limit

No more than 15% of the employees in the same department, at the same location and on the same shift, may be on vacation on the same day. Requests are counted in the order they are submitted. If a day is full, choose different dates.

### 6.7 Days in a row

You may take up to 10 scheduled workdays of vacation in a row. Longer periods need approval from your HR team.

### 6.8 Enough vacation time

Your available vacation, minus any requests already pending, must cover the request. If you do not have enough, the request cannot be submitted. Your HR team can confirm your available hours.

### 6.9 Carryover

Up to 5 unused vacation days (40 hours, or 50 hours for drivers) carry over into the next calendar year. Any unused vacation above that amount expires on December 31 and is not paid out.

### 6.10 Vacation when you leave the company

Unused vacation is handled according to the law of the state where you work (Section 19).

## 7. Requesting and Approving Time Off

Vacation is requested through the Roost HR Assistant and needs two approvals: your supervisor or manager, then your HR team.

### 7.1 How to request

Request vacation through the Roost HR Assistant in Microsoft Teams or at a plant kiosk (Section 18). The assistant checks the rules in Section 6 and shows you a summary. Your request is submitted only after you confirm it.

### 7.2 Who approves

| Employee group | First approval | Second approval |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Plant employees (Weekly Production, Drivers, plant IT Help Desk) | Supervisor or manager | Plant HR |
| Corporate employees (Executive, Biweekly, corporate IT Help Desk) | Supervisor | Corporate HR |
| CEO | None; the CEO notifies Corporate HR | None |

Approvers receive requests as Microsoft Teams messages and approve or deny them there.

### 7.3 Being notified of a decision

You are notified when your request is approved or denied. Employees with Teams receive a Teams message; employees with company email receive an email. Drivers can check the status at a plant kiosk or ask their plant HR team in person.

### 7.4 If there is no decision

If your request has not been decided within 3 business days, or 2 days before your first day off (whichever comes first), you will be emailed to check with your HR team in person. Drivers will see this notice at the kiosk. Requests are not escalated automatically.

### 7.5 Changing or cancelling a request

You may change a request after submitting it, even after it is approved. A changed request goes back through both approvals. You may cancel a request any time before your first day off; your approvers are notified.

### 7.6 Unplanned absences

If you cannot come to work without notice, follow the call-out procedure in Section 13.2. Unplanned absences are not vacation requests.

## 8. Sick Leave

Every employee receives 2 paid sick days a year, separate from vacation.

### 8.1 Amount

You receive 2 sick days (16 hours, or 20 hours for drivers) on January 1 each year. New employees receive the full amount when hired. Sick days can be used after the 90-day introductory period.

### 8.2 Uses

Sick days may be used for your own illness, injury or medical appointment, or to care for an ill family member.

### 8.3 Notice

Tell your supervisor as early as possible, and at least 1 hour before your shift. Weekly Production and Drivers must also call the plant attendance line (Section 13.2).

### 8.4 No carryover

Unused sick days do not carry over to the next year and are not paid out.

### 8.5 Longer illnesses

If you will miss more than 3 days in a row, contact your HR team. You may be eligible for FMLA leave (Section 11) or short-term disability (Section 14.6).

### 8.6 Attendance points

Approved sick days do not add attendance points (Section 13).

## 9. Bereavement Leave

Employees receive 2 paid days off when an immediate family member dies.

### 9.1 Who is covered

Immediate family means your spouse, child, parent, sibling, grandparent, or a parent, sibling or child of your spouse (in-laws). Step-relationships are treated the same as these relationships.

### 9.2 Amount

You receive up to 2 paid scheduled workdays for each death. Bereavement pay is at your regular rate for your scheduled hours.

### 9.3 How to request

Tell your supervisor as soon as you can, then contact your HR team. HR may ask for reasonable documentation, such as an obituary or funeral program.

### 9.4 Extra time

If you need more time, you may use vacation (Section 6) with your supervisor's approval, even with less than 14 days' notice. Bereavement days do not add attendance points (Section 13).

## 10. Jury Duty

Employees called for jury duty are paid their regular rate for scheduled hours missed.

### 10.1 Pay

The company pays your regular base rate for scheduled hours you miss while serving on a jury, for up to 10 workdays per calendar year. You may keep any fee the court pays you.

### 10.2 What to do

Give your supervisor a copy of your jury summons as soon as you receive it. When your service ends, give your HR team proof of attendance from the court.

### 10.3 Returning to work

If you are released from jury duty with 4 or more hours left in your scheduled shift, contact your supervisor about returning to work that day.

### 10.4 Attendance and protection

Jury duty does not add attendance points (Section 13). You will not be penalized for serving on a jury. State-specific rules are in Section 19.

## 11. Family and Medical Leave (FMLA)

Eligible employees may take up to 12 weeks of unpaid, job-protected leave in a 12-month period under the federal Family and Medical Leave Act.

### 11.1 Eligibility

Under federal law, you are generally eligible if you have worked for the company for at least 12 months, worked at least 1,250 hours in the 12 months before leave starts, and work at a location with at least 50 employees within 75 miles. Your HR team decides whether you are eligible.

### 11.2 Qualifying reasons

- The birth of a child, or placement of a child for adoption or foster care
- Caring for a spouse, child or parent with a serious health condition
- Your own serious health condition that makes you unable to work
- Certain needs related to a family member's military service

### 11.3 How to request

Contact your HR team at least 30 days before planned leave, or as soon as possible if the need is unexpected. HR will give you the required notices and forms. Medical certification may be required.

### 11.4 Pay and benefits during leave

FMLA leave is unpaid. You may choose to use available vacation or sick time during FMLA leave. Your health coverage continues during leave on the same terms, and you continue paying your share of premiums.

### 11.5 Returning to work

When FMLA leave ends, you will be returned to the same or an equivalent job. Approved FMLA leave does not add attendance points (Section 13).

### 11.6 Questions

FMLA eligibility depends on your individual situation. The Roost HR Assistant can explain this section, but only your HR team can confirm eligibility or approve FMLA leave.

### 12.5 Holidays during vacation

If an observed holiday falls during your approved vacation, it is paid as a holiday and not deducted from your vacation hours.

## 13. Attendance and Punctuality

Weekly Production employees and Drivers follow a points-based attendance policy; reaching 10 points in 12 months leads to a termination review.

### 13.1 Who this applies to

This points policy applies to Weekly Production employees and Drivers. All other employees are expected to report absences to their supervisor, and attendance concerns are handled by their supervisor and HR.

### 13.2 Calling out

If you will be absent or late, call your plant attendance line at least 1 hour before your shift starts. Leave your name, employee number, shift, and the reason. Telling a coworker is not enough.

### 13.3 Points

| Event | Points |
| --- | --- |
| No-call/no-show (absent without calling out) | 3 |
| Absent, called out less than 1 hour before shift | 1.5 |
| Absent, called out at least 1 hour before shift | 1 |
| Late arrival or early departure, under 2 hours | 0.5 |

A late arrival or early departure of 2 hours or more counts as an absence.

### 13.4 Absences that add no points

Approved vacation, approved sick days, bereavement leave, jury duty, approved FMLA leave, a work-related injury covered by workers' compensation, and company holidays do not add points.

### 13.5 Removing points

Each point expires 12 months after the date it was given. Every 90 days of perfect attendance (no points) removes 1 additional point.

### 13.6 Corrective action

| Points in a rolling 12 months | Action |
| --- | --- |
| 5 | Verbal warning |
| 7 | Written warning |
| 9 | Final written warning |
| 10 | Termination review |

### 13.7 Job abandonment

Three no-call/no-shows in a row are treated as a voluntary resignation.

### 13.8 Checking your points

To see your current point total, ask your supervisor or plant HR team. The Roost HR Assistant explains this policy but does not show point totals.

## 14. Benefits

Health coverage begins the first of the month after 45 days of employment; retirement and company-paid benefits begin after 1 year.

### 14.1 Eligibility at a glance

| Accident, critical illness | 1st of the month after 45 days | Employee premiums |

| AD&D insurance | After 1 year | Company |
| Short-term disability | After 1 year | Company |
| Voluntary accident add-on | After 1 year | Employee |
| Voluntary spouse life insurance | After 1 year | Employee |

Benefits apply to all payroll classes.

### 14.2 When coverage starts

Health coverage starts on the first day of the month after you complete 45 days of employment. For example, if your 45th day is March 10, coverage starts April 1. You must enroll within 30 days of becoming eligible.

### 14.3 Medical, dental and vision plans

Medical, dental and vision each come in three plan levels — Bronze, Silver and Gold — and three coverage levels: Single, Single + Spouse, or Single + Family. Gold plans have the highest premiums and the lowest out-of-pocket costs; Bronze plans have the lowest premiums and the highest out-of-pocket costs.

### 14.4 Accident and critical illness plans

Accident and critical illness insurance each have one plan, offered at Single, Single + Spouse, or Single + Family coverage. These plans pay cash benefits after a covered accident or diagnosis.

### 14.5 Premiums

Premiums are deducted from each paycheck. The amounts below are per biweekly paycheck. Employees paid weekly have half of these amounts deducted from each weekly paycheck.

### 14.6 Short-term disability and AD&D

After 1 year, the company provides short-term disability (STD) insurance and accidental death and dismemberment (AD&D) insurance at no cost to you. STD replaces part of your pay if a non-work illness or injury keeps you from working. Contact your HR team to file an STD claim.

### 14.7 401(k) and Roth 401(k)

After 1 year, you may contribute to a traditional 401(k), a Roth 401(k), or both. The company matches 100% of the first 4% of pay you contribute.

### 14.8 Voluntary benefits

After 1 year, you may buy a voluntary accident add-on (in addition to the accident plan in Section 14.4) and voluntary spouse life insurance. You pay the full cost through payroll deduction.

### 14.9 Enrollment and changes

You can change your elections during open enrollment each November, for coverage starting January 1. Outside open enrollment, you can change elections within 30 days of a qualifying life event, such as marriage, divorce, birth or adoption, or a spouse's loss of coverage.

### 14.10 Benefits questions

For questions about your elections, deductions or claims, contact your HR team (Section 2.3). The Roost HR Assistant can explain this section but cannot show your elections or deductions.

## 15. Equal Opportunity, Harassment and Retaliation

The company does not tolerate discrimination, harassment or retaliation, and every report is taken seriously.

### 15.1 Equal employment opportunity

Employment decisions are based on qualifications and job performance. The company does not discriminate based on race, color, religion, sex (including pregnancy, sexual orientation and gender identity), national origin, age, disability, genetic information, veteran status, or any other status protected by law.

### 15.2 Harassment

Harassment based on any protected status is prohibited. This includes unwelcome comments, jokes, slurs, gestures, images, touching, and requests for sexual favors. It applies at work, at company events, and in messages between coworkers.

### 15.3 Reasonable accommodation

The company provides reasonable accommodations for qualified employees with disabilities, for pregnancy-related needs, and for sincerely held religious beliefs, unless doing so would cause undue hardship. To request one, contact your HR team.

### 15.4 How to report

If you experience or see harassment or discrimination, report it to your supervisor, your HR team (Section 2.3), or the Ethics Hotline at 1-800-555-0199. You do not have to report to someone involved in the behavior.

### 15.5 Investigation

HR will investigate reports promptly and keep them as confidential as possible. Employees who violate this policy face corrective action, up to and including termination.

### 15.6 No retaliation

Retaliation against anyone who reports a concern in good faith or takes part in an investigation is strictly prohibited. Report retaliation the same way as in Section 15.4.

## 16. Safety and Injury Reporting

Report every injury and safety hazard immediately; in an emergency, call 911 first.

### 16.1 Emergencies

In a medical emergency, fire, chemical release or threat of violence, call 911, then notify your supervisor and the plant safety manager. Follow your plant's posted evacuation routes.

### 16.2 Reporting an injury

Report any work-related injury or illness, however minor, to your supervisor and the plant safety manager before the end of your shift. Reporting promptly protects your right to workers' compensation benefits.

### 16.3 Workers' compensation

Employees injured on the job may be eligible for workers' compensation, which covers medical care and part of lost wages. The plant safety manager and your HR team will help you file a claim.

### 16.4 Safety hazards

Report unsafe conditions, such as a wet floor, a missing machine guard or an ammonia smell, to your supervisor or the plant safety manager right away. You may also report anonymously through the Ethics Hotline.

### 16.5 Personal protective equipment

Wear all required personal protective equipment (PPE) for your area, such as gloves, hairnets, boots, hearing protection and cut-resistant sleeves. Replacement PPE is provided free from your supervisor.

### 16.6 Your right to a safe workplace

You have the right to report safety concerns without retaliation, including to the Occupational Safety and Health Administration (OSHA).

## 17. Reporting Concerns and the Ethics Hotline

You can raise any concern with your HR team, or anonymously through the Ethics Hotline at 1-800-555-0199, any time.

### 17.1 What to report

Report harassment, discrimination, retaliation, safety hazards, workplace violence or threats, pay concerns, fraud, theft, food safety violations, or any other conduct you believe breaks the law or company policy.

### 17.2 Where to report

- Your supervisor or manager
- Your HR team (Section 2.3)
- The Ethics Hotline: 1-800-555-0199, open 24 hours a day, 7 days a week, in English, Spanish and Vietnamese

### 17.3 Anonymous reports

You may report to the Ethics Hotline without giving your name. The hotline gives you a report number so you can follow up without identifying yourself.

### 17.4 Sensitive personal matters

For personal matters such as a medical condition, an accommodation request, immigration or work authorization questions, or a personal crisis, contact your HR team directly. If you are in crisis or thinking about harming yourself, call or text 988 (Suicide and Crisis Lifeline) or call 911.

### 17.5 No retaliation

No one may be retaliated against for reporting a concern in good faith (Section 15.6).

## 18. Using the Roost HR Assistant

Roost is the company's HR assistant, available 24/7 in Microsoft Teams and at plant kiosks, in English, Spanish and Vietnamese.

### 18.1 What Roost can do

- Answer questions about this handbook and tell you which section the answer comes from
- Tell you your pay schedule and paydays
- Submit, change and cancel vacation requests (Section 7)
- Give you the right HR contact or the Ethics Hotline number

### 18.2 What Roost cannot do

- Show pay amounts, deductions, vacation or sick balances, attendance point totals, benefit elections, or bank information
- Arrange shift swaps (talk to your supervisor, Section 4.5)
- Decide whether you are eligible for leave or benefits, or give legal advice

For any of these, contact your HR team (Section 2.3).

### 18.3 How to access Roost

| Channel | How to sign in | Language |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Microsoft Teams | Automatically, with your company Microsoft account | Roost replies in the language you write in; you can ask it to switch |
| Plant kiosk | Employee number and your 6-digit PIN | Choose English, Español or Tiếng Việt on the start screen |

Kiosks sign you out after 2 minutes without activity. Always sign out when you finish. Plant HR sets your first PIN in person, and you must choose a new PIN the first time you sign in. Your PIN must be 6 digits and cannot be a simple pattern (such as 123456 or 000000) or your birth date. After 5 wrong attempts, your account is locked for 15 minutes. Never share your PIN; if you forget it, ask your plant HR team to reset it in person.

### 18.4 Drivers

Drivers do not have company Teams or email accounts. Drivers can use the kiosk at their assigned plant, and should check with their plant HR team in person about request decisions.

### 18.5 Sensitive matters

For harassment, discrimination, retaliation, injuries, safety hazards, medical or accommodation needs, immigration questions, pay disputes or a personal crisis, Roost gives you your HR team's contact information and the Ethics Hotline number. With your permission, Roost can send your HR team a short summary so they can follow up. Roost never forwards anything to the Ethics Hotline, so hotline reports stay anonymous.

### 18.6 Privacy and records

Conversations with Roost are kept for 45 days and can be viewed only by authorized HR administrators. A conversation may be kept longer if it is needed for an investigation, a dispute or a legal requirement. Reports of sensitive matters are never used for analytics or system testing. Vacation requests and approvals are kept permanently as part of your employment records.

### 18.7 Roost's answers

Roost's answers are for information only. If an answer differs from this handbook, this handbook applies. All final decisions are made by HR. Answers in Spanish or Vietnamese are translations; the English handbook is binding (Section 1.4).

## 19. State Supplements

These supplements add location-specific rules for each state where the company operates. Where a supplement differs from the main handbook, the supplement applies to employees in that state.

### 19.1 All locations: vacation at separation

Employees who leave the company and give at least 2 weeks' notice are paid for unused, earned vacation on their final paycheck. Employees terminated for misconduct, or who leave without 2 weeks' notice, are not paid for unused vacation unless state law requires it.

### 19.2 Georgia Plant

- **Voting leave:** Employees may take up to 2 hours of paid time to vote in a public election if the polls are not open for at least 2 hours before or after their shift. Tell your supervisor at least 1 day in advance.
- **Jury duty:** In addition to Section 10, employees are not required to use vacation for any day of jury service.

### 19.3 Arkansas Plant

- **Voting leave:** The company schedules work so every employee has time to vote on election day. If your shift prevents it, tell your supervisor, who will adjust your schedule.
- **Jury duty:** Section 10 applies. Employees may not be required to use vacation or sick days for jury service.

### 19.4 North Carolina Plant

- **School involvement leave:** Parents and guardians of school-age children may take up to 4 hours of unpaid leave per year to attend school activities, with at least 48 hours' notice to their supervisor. Employees may use vacation instead to be paid.
- **Voting leave:** Same as Section 19.2.
- **Jury duty:** Section 10 applies.

### 19.5 Corporate Office (Texas)

- **Voting leave:** Employees may take paid time off to vote if the polls are not open for at least 2 consecutive hours outside their working hours.
- **Jury duty:** Section 10 applies. Employees are paid for each day of jury service up to the Section 10 limit.
